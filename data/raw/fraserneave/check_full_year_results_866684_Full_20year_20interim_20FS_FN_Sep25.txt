2.    Basis of preparation

      The condensed interim financial statements for the six months and the year ended 30 September 2025 have been
      prepared in accordance with Singapore Financial Reporting Standards (International) (“SFRS(I)”) 1-34 Interim Financial
      Reporting issued by the Accounting Standards Council Singapore. The condensed interim financial statements do not
      include all the information required for a complete set of financial statements. However, selected explanatory notes are
      included to explain events and transactions that are significant to an understanding of the changes in the Group’s financial
      position and performance of the Group since the audited financial statements for the year ended 30 September 2024.

      The condensed interim financial statements are presented in Singapore Dollar and all values are rounded to the nearest
      thousand ($’000) unless otherwise stated.

      The Group and the Company have applied the same accounting policies and methods of computation in the preparation
      of the interim financial statements and are consistent with those used in the Group’s financial statements for the year
      ended 30 September 2024 which were prepared in accordance with SFRS(I)s except for the adoption of various new
      and amended accounting standards which are effective from 1 October 2024. The adoption of these new and amended
      accounting standards did not have a material impact on the financial statements of the Group and the Company.

      In addition, the Group has applied SFRS(I) 1-41 Agriculture for the first time upon commencement of a new dairy farm
      in Malaysia during the financial year.

      SFRS(I) 1-41 Agriculture

      (a)   Dairy Herd and Crops

            The Group’s biological assets comprise dairy herd and crops. Biological assets are measured on initial recognition
            and at the end of the reporting date at their fair value less costs to sell. Changes in fair values are recognised in
            profit or loss in the period in which they arise.

            Feed and other related costs are capitalised until such time as the heifers begin to produce milk, bulls and male
            calves are ready for sale or crops are ready for harvest.

       (a) Agricultural produce

            Agricultural produce, such as milk and harvested crops, is measured at fair value less costs to sell at the point of
            milking or harvest. Such measurement is considered the deemed cost for the purposes of applying SFRS(I) 1-2,
            Inventories.

            Gain or loss on initial recognition of agricultural produce is recognised in profit or loss in the period in which they
            arise.

2.      Review of performance of the Group

        Statement of profit or loss for the 12 months ended 30 September 2025

        Revenue increased 7.4% to $2,322.8 million, an increase of $160.7 million from $2,162.1 million last year. PBIT of
        $308.1 million was an increase of 3.7% from $297.0 million while attributable profit before fair value adjustment and
        exceptional items remained stable at $150.4 million.


        Beverages

        Revenue from Beverages increased by $103.8 million to $772.1 million, 15.5% higher than last year mainly from Beer
        operations in Myanmar as the Group’s TAPPER beer, launched in mid-2024, continues to gain traction and drive higher
        sales volume. Growth in Soft Drinks revenue was supported by higher water volumes and new product launches.

        Underpinned by the increase in revenue, PBIT improved 5.8% to $40.1 million. While Beer achieved stronger
        profitability on the back of higher sales and reduced operating expenses, Soft Drinks benefitted from favourable cost
        environment, operational efficiencies and positive forex translation.


        Dairies

        Dairies revenue increased by $69.0 million, a 5.7% increase to $1,276.4 million for the year mainly due to higher Dairies
        sales in Thailand and Malaysia which were lifted by strong export and domestic canned milk sales in key markets and
        positive forex translation. Commencement of commercial milking operations at F&N AgriValley alongside maiden
        contribution from the Malaysia School Milk Programme also supported the revenue increase.

        Dairies PBIT grew 6.1% to $262.1 million propelled by higher sales, favourable input costs environment, disciplined
        advertising and promotion spend and positive forex translation. The increase in PBIT was moderated by start-up
        expenses incurred for the dairy farm and the decline in share of results from the Group’s associated company in
        Vietnam, Vietnam Dairy Products Joint Stock Company (“Vinamilk”).


        Printing and Publishing

        Printing and Publishing (“P&P”) revenue decreased 2.3% to $196.6 million primarily due to the absence of one-off title
        sales and licensing contracts recorded by the publishing business last year. The decline was partly mitigated by
        increased production volumes achieved by the sustainable print packaging business, driven by global supply chain
        diversification and rising demand for eco-friendly paper packaging.

        Losses increased from $1.5 million to $5.5 million attributed to lower contributions from Education and Print businesses
        which were impacted by absence of last year’s one-off sales and unfavourable input costs environment.


        Tax

        The Group’s effective tax rate (“ETR”) of 20.6 % (FY 2024: 18.0 %) can be attributed to the decrease in non-taxable
        income following the expiry of a tax incentive in one of the subsidiaries in Thailand.

2.      Review of performance of the Group (cont’d)

        Statement of financial position as at 30 September 2025

        increase in property, plant and equipment was mainly due to capital expenditure incurred in relation to the integrated
        dairy farm in Malaysia and dairy plant in Cambodia offset by the decrease in investment in associates, due to the impact
        of unfavourable translation of the Group’s interest in Vinamilk partly cushioned by the share of results of Vinamilk.

        Current assets decreased by $182.8 million or 13.7% from $1,336.0 million to $1,153.2 million. This was mainly due to
        the decrease in cash balances, as a result of capital expenditure incurred and repayment of borrowings, and decrease
        in other receivables due to subsequent receipt of dividend receivable outstanding as at 30 September 2024.

        Liabilities decreased by $108.1 million or 5.9% from $1,821.7 million to $1,713.6 million mainly due to repayment of
        bank borrowings.


        Statement of cash flows for the 12 months ended 30 September 2025

        The cash inflows and outflows are detailed in the Group Cash Flow Statement.

        Net cash inflow due to operating activities for the year was $231.2 million as compared to $216.4 million mainly due to
        the increase in cash generated from operations partly offset by higher income tax payments.

        Net cash outflow due to investing activities of $191.1 million was higher as compared to $20.0 million last year mainly
        due to increased capital expenditure incurred for the dairy farm in Malaysia and dairy plant in Cambodia, purchase of
        livestock for the dairy farm and decrease in dividend received from an associate due to timing of dividend declared.

        Net cash outflow due to financing activities for the period was $201.5 million as compared to $119.1 million last year
        due to net repayment of borrowings during the year.

        increased contribution from Dairies and Beverages as a result of successful promotional campaigns and new product
        launches.

        Supported by the increase in revenue, PBIT increased 6.4% to $143.0 million as compared to the corresponding period
        last year. This was mainly driven by increased profitability in Dairies as a result of higher sales, lower input costs and
        improved cost efficiencies.

        As compared to the corresponding period last year, group attributable profit before fair value adjustment and exceptional
        items remained flat at $66.3 million.

4.      A commentary at the date of the announcement of the significant trends and competitive conditions of the
        industry in which the Group operates and any known factors or events that may affect the Group in the next
        reporting period and the next 12 months.

        Amidst the on-going global trade and geopolitical tensions and financial markets volatility, the Group continued to build
        resilience across its business units. Driven by strong brand execution, innovation and cost discipline, our businesses
        have continued to perform well. These efforts, together with the benefit of favourable cost and currency environment
        helped the Group deliver growth in revenue and earnings despite regional operational challenges, broader economic
        headwinds and cautious consumer sentiment.

        In Malaysia, during the year, the Group’s integrated dairy farm continues to make strong progress and remains on track
        towards commercial scale-up. The farm welcomed its first commercial herd of 2,500 dairy cattle in April 2025 and herd
        expansion has since progressed well. It now houses over 6,500 cattle of which 2,500 dairy cattle have just arrived and
        are currently under quarantine. Pilot milking operations commenced in mid-2025 and the adjacent dairy manufacturing
        facility is on track to begin commercial production in Q1 2026, which will be marketed under the F&N MAGNOLIA brand.
        The project remains on track towards commercial scale-up, strengthening the Group’s upstream integration and
        supporting F&N’s commitment to sustainable business practices.

        In Cambodia, the Group continues to strengthen its regional manufacturing capabilities with the development of a new
        dairy manufacturing facility. Construction of the plant is progressing as planned, with operations expected to commence
        early 2026.

        In the P&P segment, the Group remains focused on driving operational improvements and repositioning the business
        for sustainable recovery. While the Group continues to accelerate the scaling up of Green Lab’s operations to
        turnaround Print operations, we also strive to deepen Education’s international reach and strengthen Book Distribution.
        These efforts, coupled with disciplined cost management and targeted investment in growth segments, are expected
        to build long-term resilience and sustainable success.

        As the Group looks ahead, we will continue to strengthen our brands, leverage digital and sustainability initiatives and
        pursue opportunities that drives growth and create lasting value for our shareholders.
